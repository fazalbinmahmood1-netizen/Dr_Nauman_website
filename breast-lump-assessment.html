Found a breast lump? What to expect at an assessment

A new breast or armpit lump deserves a clinical examination, even if it feels small or does not hurt. Many lumps have benign causes. Touch alone cannot reliably tell which one you have. Arrange an appointment promptly and mention the new lump when booking so the clinic understands why you are calling.

What to mention when you book

Say which side is affected, when you first noticed it and whether you have seen a change in the skin or nipple. Mention dimpling, a nipple turning inward or bloodstained discharge. If you have had breast imaging or treatment before, bring the dated reports and images you already have.

If privacy is a concern

You can ask the clinic about arrangements for the examination, including a chaperone, before travelling. If you want a relative to come with you, decide beforehand which parts of the conversation you would like them to join. Write down your main question so it is easier to raise even if the appointment feels stressful.

Why more than one test may be offered

Assessment can include examination, ultrasound or mammography. Sometimes the team recommends a needle biopsy to collect tissue for testing. A request for a biopsy means the clinician needs more information. Ask which finding is being sampled and who will explain the result to you.

Close the loop on results

Before leaving, ask whether another appointment is already booked or whether you need to call. Write down the expected date and contact number. If an earlier scan was reassuring but you have since noticed a new change, explain what is different and when it appeared. Bring the old report so the clinician can compare the findings in context.
