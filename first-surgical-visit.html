Preparing for your first surgical visit

A referral to a general surgeon can leave you wondering whether an operation has already been decided. Your first visit is a chance to explain what has been happening, have an examination and understand the choices ahead. A little preparation can make that conversation easier, especially if your reports have come from several hospitals.

Start with the problem that brought you in

Try describing the first episode in your own words. Where were you when it started? What made you stop what you were doing? Explain what has changed since then. “I used to finish a shift standing, but now I need to sit down after lunch” tells the surgeon something a pain score alone cannot.

Put your papers in an order you can explain

Bring the referral, test reports, scan images and any discharge summary from a previous operation. Keep the newest report on top and mark which problem each test was for. If a relative keeps your results on their phone, check before travelling that the files open and the dates are visible. Bring the originals you already have. Ask the clinic before paying to repeat a test.

Give the surgeon the full picture

Bring a list of medicines, doses, supplements and allergies. If you cannot remember a name, take the packet or a clear photograph. Mention past operations and any problems with anaesthesia. Tell the surgeon which concern matters most to you, whether that is pain at work, looking after a child or understanding an unexpected scan result.

Leave with one clear next step

Before you leave, say back what you understood. For example, “I will bring the ultrasound report next time. Who should I call if the swelling changes before then?” Write down who will arrange the test, when to return and how you will receive results. If you are travelling from Faisalabad or Lahore for a planned visit, confirm the Toba Tek Singh clinic and session before setting off.
