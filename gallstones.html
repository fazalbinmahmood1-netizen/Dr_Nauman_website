Gallstones on ultrasound: when is gallbladder surgery needed?

An ultrasound report that mentions gallstones can raise two different questions. Did the stones cause the pain that led to the scan? And would removing the gallbladder help? People in Pakistan sometimes call this pittay ki pathri. Take the report and a description of your symptoms to the consultation so the surgeon can consider both questions together.

When to seek urgent care

Sudden severe abdominal pain, pain spreading to the back with vomiting, fever with shivering, or yellow eyes or skin needs emergency assessment. Seek help at the nearest emergency department. A planned clinic appointment is appropriate for discussing a stable finding once urgent symptoms have been addressed.

What an ultrasound can tell you

Some stones are found during a scan done for another reason. Stones that have caused no symptoms may need no treatment. Stones in the main bile duct are a different finding. Your clinician may use blood tests alongside imaging to understand what is happening.

Describe an attack from beginning to end

Think back to the most recent episode. Point to where the pain began. Estimate how long it lasted and say whether it stopped completely between episodes. Mention any vomiting, fever or colour change in your eyes. If you have had several visits, take the reports from those visits and match them to the dates of the attacks.

Discussing gallbladder removal

When stones cause symptoms or complications, the surgeon may discuss removing the gallbladder. The operation is called a cholecystectomy. Ask which findings support that recommendation in your case and what the plan is if a duct stone is suspected. If you are comparing estimates, write the exact procedure name beside each estimate. Before leaving, ask which symptoms should bring you back sooner and when to return if the pain settles.
