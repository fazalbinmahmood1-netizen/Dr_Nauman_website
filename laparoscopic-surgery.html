Laparoscopic surgery: preparing for keyhole surgery and recovery

If you have been offered “camera surgery,” ask for the full name of the operation. Laparoscopy describes the way the surgeon reaches the abdomen or pelvis. It can be used to investigate a problem or to treat one. Recovery after a diagnostic look can differ considerably from recovery after an organ has been removed.

What happens in theatre?

During laparoscopy, the patient usually has a general anaesthetic. The surgeon places a camera through a small opening and uses gas to create space to see. Further openings may be needed for instruments. Ask what treatment your surgeon expects to carry out and what might lead the team to change the approach.

Prepare for your particular operation

Get the hospital’s fasting and medicine instructions in writing. If you take a blood thinner or diabetes medicine, ask the team for an individual plan before changing anything. Arrange an adult to take you home. If your family member is coming from another city, check where the hospital expects them to wait and who will call when you are ready.

Plan recovery around activities

Ask about washing, dressings, walking, stairs and getting back to your work. Give examples of the tasks you actually do. A desk-based job, riding a motorcycle and lifting a child each raise different practical questions. Keep your discharge note where the person helping you can find it.

When recovery takes an unexpected turn

Get prompt medical advice for increasing abdominal pain, fever, repeated vomiting or redness spreading around a wound. Chest pain or serious difficulty breathing needs emergency care. Before leaving hospital, write down a daytime and an out-of-hours contact, when results will be available and the date of your follow-up appointment.
