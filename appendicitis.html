Appendicitis symptoms: when abdominal pain needs urgent attention

Pain that keeps getting worse, fails to settle or moves towards the lower right abdomen needs urgent assessment. Severe abdominal pain, confusion or difficulty breathing warrants emergency care immediately. If appendicitis is a possibility, go to a nearby hospital rather than travelling to a preferred surgeon’s routine clinic.

How the pain can change

The appendix is a small part of the bowel. When it becomes inflamed, pain often begins around the belly button and later settles towards the lower right. Some people have a different pattern. You do not need to wait for the pain to move before seeking help. Other conditions can cause similar symptoms and may also be urgent.

What to tell the receiving team

Begin with the time the person was last comfortable. Explain when pain began, where it is now and whether movement makes it worse. If you are accompanying a child, describe what you observed as well as what the child said. Mention vomiting, any medicines already taken and the possibility of pregnancy where relevant. Gathering papers should never delay the journey.

Why the hospital may use several tests

An examination helps the team decide whether blood or urine tests, ultrasound or CT would help. A pregnancy test may be relevant because other urgent conditions can cause similar pain. The tests are interpreted together with changes in symptoms. If the team recommends observation, ask who will reassess you and how to report increasing pain.

Before going home

If you are discharged after assessment, ask what the team thinks the pain could be, which changes require immediate return and where to go overnight. Keep your discharge note and test results together. A reassuring assessment earlier in the day is still worth mentioning if you return because the pain has changed.
